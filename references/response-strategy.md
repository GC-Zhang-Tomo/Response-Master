# Response strategy and manuscript-wide revision

Use this guidance when comments question the central claim, novelty, comparability of experiments, sample preparation, statistics, or figure evidence. Historical examples demonstrate response strategies, not verified scientific facts or universal experimental protocols.

## Understand the editorial decision

Distinguish an invited revision from rejection with possible consideration as a new submission, an appeal, or transfer. Use the supplied decision letter; never imply acceptance or an invitation that the editor did not give. Address substantive editor comments before reviewer comments, including general assessments and significance-specific comments outside numbered questions.

An opening reply to an editor or a reviewer's general assessment may summarize the main changes briefly. The numbered replies still need evidence and locations for each request. When the separate original review is unavailable and comments are embedded in a historical response, preserve the embedded text but mark completeness against the original letter as unverified.

When a cover letter is supplied, check the current title, prior manuscript identifier, submission route, summary of major changes, and list of enclosed files against the decision letter and actual package. Distinguish the historical title from the current title. Use it for a concise overview of supported changes, not as evidence that experiments occurred or that the editor invited revision. Flag conflicting accounts of editorial permission for author clarification. A cover letter is optional supporting material; create or revise one only when requested.

## Choose an evidence-supported response

For each concern, decide whether the appropriate action is a factual correction, clarification of existing work, new experiment or analysis, narrower conclusion, or reasoned disagreement. Several actions may be needed for one question.

- Identify the reviewer's underlying uncertainty, including an alternative explanation the original design did not exclude.
- State what each available control or complementary method addresses and what remains unresolved. Multiple methods do not automatically prove a stronger claim than their combined scope permits.
- If using an alternative to the requested method, explain why it addresses the same concern and disclose its limitations; naming the alternative alone is not an answer.
- If a reviewer misread an ambiguous method, acknowledge the ambiguity, establish the correct account from records, and put the clarification into the manuscript where readers need it.
- Acknowledging prior work should lead to an accurate account of the remaining contribution. Do not invent a new novelty claim or dismiss relevant prior studies.
- When several reviewers raise the same issue, link them to one change/evidence record. Give a short direct answer in each reply and cross-reference a fully identified reviewer/comment for detail. Do not answer only with “see above.”

## Propagate changes in claims

For each central claim under challenge, record the original assertion, measured endpoint, supported replacement, remaining limitation, and all affected locations. Distinguish a proxy readout from the mechanism or downstream outcome it is being used to suggest, and local resemblance from equivalence of an entire system.

Review the title, abstract, significance statement when present, introduction, results, discussion/limitations, section headings, figure titles, legends, schematics, and SI. Update affected author claims consistently, but do not mechanically replace wording in quoted reviewer comments or reference titles. Do not add a journal-specific section to papers that do not have or require it.

Use the narrower conclusion in the response too. A new assay that measures a related endpoint may strengthen a reply without satisfying the original stronger claim; state that boundary explicitly.

## Trace samples and compare conditions

Where preparation history affects interpretation, map source material → preparation or treatment → resulting sample → assay/figure. Distinguish the material characterized structurally from later preparations used functionally. Record whether identity or comparability between those preparations was demonstrated, assumed, or remains to be checked.

For comparisons, check source/species, construct or composition, preparation batch, buffer, handling, labeling/treatment, reaction vessel and volume, temperature, duration, agitation, dose and dose basis wherever relevant. Equal nominal concentrations need not mean equal amounts of the entity being compared. Specify mass, molar, particle, or monomer-equivalent normalization, calibration method, and assumptions for mixtures.

Changes in units, species, constructs, dose, or treatment conditions between drafts are factual changes, not automatic copyediting. Require experimental records or author confirmation, log the correction and its consequences, and never infer a conversion or a newly performed experiment from revised prose alone. Do not copy historical numerical conditions into another manuscript.

## Explain the statistical unit

For every quantitative panel, identify what one point represents, the reported n, independent biological preparations, technical subsamples, grouping/nesting, normalization denominator, summary measure, error bars, and statistical comparison as applicable.

An increased number of images, fields, particles, or cells is not automatically an increased number of independent biological replicates. If the hierarchy is unclear, flag the inference and request the design information; do not invent independence or choose a new test without an adequate design basis.

A representative image or blot can support a qualitative observation but does not replace missing replicate quantification. If a quantitative panel is removed or replaced, explain how the revised evidence addresses the original concern and what remains unanswered.

## Close figure and method changes

Maintain an old-to-new figure/panel mapping and a separate response-figure-to-manuscript/SI mapping. Panel relabeling must propagate to text callouts, legends, response captions, and schematics. Reused evidence may have several response references; keep its identity and final destination consistent.

For each new experiment, consider Results text, reproducible Methods, figure/table or SI, full legend, statistics, source data, and citations. Record applicable destinations and mark unavailable assets as unverified rather than claiming they were updated.

Inspect supplied SI itself, including figure artwork, panel labels, legends, and tables; a response's statement that something was added does not verify its presence. Cross-check shared numerical values, units, sample labels, and table row/column associations against Methods, legends, and replies. Different counts or processing parameters may describe different stages: identify the stage and denominator from records before treating them as equivalent or selecting a value. Distinguish the largest individual class from a majority and record what a reported percentage counts. Check scale-bar values and statistical definitions in the figure or legend, not just the existence of a caption.

If only revised SI is available, verify its current contents but mark before/after SI changes and red-text completeness as unverified. Keep optional-file absence separate from a contradiction found in a supplied file; log the latter with both locations for author resolution.

For a questioned image feature, directly explain the feature or acknowledge that its identity is unresolved. Improved readability alone does not answer a request to explain a band, density, artifact, or impurity. Document whether an updated panel comes from a new experiment, a new representative field, reanalysis, or display-only editing; preserve source provenance. Specify scale-bar values and processing details where needed rather than relying on a generic assurance.

## Finish from the actual revised files

Treat response quotations as proposed wording until inserted. For final delivery, extract the passage from the actual destination file, including passages spanning several paragraphs. Compare body text and figure captions as well as Methods. A missing match may reflect a stale quote, citation rendering, or an unavailable SI file; investigate instead of rewriting facts to force a match.

Check substantive differences between baseline and revision independently of text color. Red text can be incomplete, and a red paragraph can contain unchanged text. Record deletions, moved sections, and unrelated factual changes separately. Author lists, affiliations, contributions, funding, and declarations require explicit author-supplied instructions and must not be inferred from new experiments.
